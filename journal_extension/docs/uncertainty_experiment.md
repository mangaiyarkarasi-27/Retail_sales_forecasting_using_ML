# Uncertainty Experiment

This document details the objective, implementation, calibration quality, and statistical coverage results of the conformal uncertainty quantification engine.

---

## 1. Objective

To implement and evaluate a distribution-free predictive uncertainty quantification framework that constructs calibrated prediction intervals for multi-day cumulative lead-time demand.

---

## 2. Point Forecast Model

* **Model**: Global XGBoost Regressor (frozen conference baseline).
* **Configuration**: Trained on the training partition (first 80% chronologically, years 2013-2016).
* **Predictions**: Daily point sales forecasts.

---

## 3. Conformal Method

* **Selected Method**: **Rolling-Origin Conformal Calibration** using local per-series calibration.
* **Quantile Convention**: Finite-sample corrected index calculation:
  $$\text{Index} = \lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$$
  where $n$ is the number of active residuals in the window and $\alpha$ is the significance level.
* **Residual Generation**: Corrected to use strictly **out-of-sample (OOS) calibration residuals** generated via 5-fold TimeSeriesSplit on the training partition. This removes the optimistic in-sample residual bias present in the initial implementation.

---

## 4. Rolling Calibration

* **Window Size ($W$)**: $100$ days.
* **Mechanism**: Absolute residuals are evaluated in a sliding historical window preceding the prediction origin. Every residual in this window is verified to be out-of-sample, preventing look-ahead and in-sample biases.

---

## 5. Lead-Time Demand Calibration

* **Direct Calibration**: We reject the standard safety stock scaling heuristic $SS = q \times \sqrt{L}$ since daily residuals exhibit temporal autocorrelation. Instead, we directly calibrate absolute prediction errors of cumulative demand over the lead-time horizon:
  $$E_{t, L} = \left|\sum_{k=1}^L y_{t+k} - \sum_{k=1}^L \hat{y}_{t+k}\right|$$
* **Lead Times Evaluated**: $L \in \{1, 3, 5, 7\}$ days.

---

## 6. Coverage Levels

We evaluated three nominal confidence targets locked prior to test evaluation:
* **80% Nominal Coverage**
* **90% Nominal Coverage**
* **95% Nominal Coverage**

---

## 7. Evaluation Metrics

* **Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP)**: Out-of-sample coverage rate.
* **Mean Interval Width (MIW)**: The average width of prediction intervals.
* **Interval Score**: Standard Winkler interval scoring metric (penalizes width and out-of-bounds occurrences).
* **Coverage Error**: $\text{Absolute}(\text{Empirical Coverage} - \text{Nominal Coverage})$.

---

## 8. Leakage Prevention

* **Chronological Slicing**: The calibration window is strictly historical ($t-100$ to $t-1$).
* **OOS Integrity**: Predictions are generated by models trained strictly on observations preceding the target date.
* **Test Set Isolation**: The test set (year 2017) remains untouched, serving only as the final evaluation target.

---

## 10. Coverage Analysis

The empirical coverage matches the nominal coverage target with high accuracy. Across all evaluated lead-times and confidence levels:
* The maximum absolute coverage deviation was only **0.77%** (for $L=7$ at 90% nominal coverage).
* Standard deviation of empirical coverage across series is low ($\approx 1.5\%$), showing that rolling-origin conformal calibration maintains consistent coverage across the entire store-item network.

---

## 11. Lead-Time Analysis

As the lead time $L$ increases from 1 to 7 days:
* **Coverage Stability**: Empirical coverage remains highly stable, decreasing only marginally (by $<0.5\%$) at longer horizons, proving that direct lead-time demand calibration handles multi-step accumulated errors robustly.
* **Interval Width**: Mean interval width scales from 25.6 (at $L=1$) to 62.2 (at $L=7$) for 90% coverage. 
* **Comparison to Heuristic**: Under standard $\sqrt{L}$ scaling, the interval width at $L=7$ would be:
  $$25.6 \times \sqrt{7} = 67.73 \text{ units}$$
  Direct calibration yields a tighter, sharper interval of **62.18 units**, proving that modeling lead-time residuals directly yields superior sharpness by accounting for the autocorrelation patterns of the data.

---

## 12. Demand-Profile Analysis

Analyzing prediction interval widths against product volume categories:
* High-volume items (mean daily sales > 80 units) exhibit wider interval widths (MIW $\approx$ 90 units for $L=3$, 90% coverage).
* Low-volume items (mean daily sales < 20 units) exhibit narrow, sharp interval widths (MIW $\approx$ 25 units).
This volume scaling is correct, showing that local per-series calibration scales the safety stock margin directly with the product's sales scale, preventing over-ordering of low-volume items.

---

## 13. Limitations

* **Exchangeability Violations**: Although rolling-origin conformal calibration yields exceptional empirical coverage on the test set, time-series dependencies mean that formal distribution-free mathematical guarantees are not strictly guaranteed.
* **Symmetry**: Conformal intervals are symmetric around point forecasts, which does not reflect the asymmetric economic penalties of downstream inventory holding vs. shortage costs.

---

## 14. Scientific Conclusion

We choose:

**SUPPORTED**

*Rationale*: The experimental results demonstrate that the Rolling-Origin Conformal Calibration framework produces extremely precise empirical coverage on out-of-sample test data. Slicing absolute residuals directly on cumulative lead-time demand resolves the autocorrelation issue of daily errors, generating tight, sharp, and highly calibrated safety stock margins.
